# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Robotics and autonomy **recruiters and hiring managers** are the primary audience — people evaluating whether to bring the owner in for a role at a robotics/autonomy company. They arrive with limited time, scanning for credibility and depth: real systems built, the hardware↔software range, and a clear sense of how this person thinks. Secondary visitors (research/lab contacts, peers in the Indian robotics community) should also find the work respectable, but the page is tuned to earn a "let's talk to this person" from a hiring reader.

The owner is a robotics & software engineer based in Bengaluru, India — B.Tech in Smart Manufacturing (IIITDM Chennai, CGPA 9.07), currently an Autonomy Intern at ATI Robotics, open to work. Core stack: ROS2, C++, Python, computer vision, SLAM, MoveIt2, Gazebo.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that establishes the owner as a serious, hireable robotics engineer who works fluently across hardware and software. It exists to convert a cold visitor into a conversation — by showing real projects (7DOF manipulation, real-image Sudoku solver, AMR trajectory tracking), a coherent intellectual arc (hardware → software → robotics → embodied AI / world models), and a credible track record (work, education, community). Success looks like a recruiter leaving convinced the owner can build autonomous systems end-to-end, and reaching out.

## Brand Personality

Thoughtful, technically deep, quietly confident. Three words: **rigorous, reflective, builder.** The voice is an engineer who has actually shipped systems and thinks carefully about why they matter — not a hype reel. It evokes the feeling of a well-instrumented lab: precise, dark, calm, with the work front and center. Copy is plain and earned ("Robots I've built, systems I've designed, code I'm proud of"), never salesy. Confidence shows through specificity and restraint, not volume.

## Anti-references

- **Flashy / over-animated portfolios.** No scroll-jacking, particle fields, or gimmicky motion competing with the work. Motion serves orientation and craft, never spectacle.
- **Overly playful / cute.** No emoji-heavy, cartoonish, or jokey tone that would undercut technical seriousness.
- **Generic dev-template portfolio.** Avoid the cookie-cutter "awesome developer portfolio" pattern (stock hero, skills progress bars, identical project-card grid).
- **Corporate/sterile SaaS marketing.** No cold blue-gradient templated energy with no person behind it.

## Design Principles

1. **The work is the argument.** Lead with real systems and specifics; let projects and outcomes do the convincing rather than adjectives.
2. **Restraint reads as confidence.** A calm, dark, precise surface signals an engineer who doesn't need to shout. Every effect must earn its place.
3. **Hardware and software are one story.** The through-line — physical machines plus the code that makes them think — is the differentiator; reinforce it everywhere.
4. **Respect the reader's time.** A scannable, well-structured surface (bento home, clear sections) that a busy recruiter can read in 30 seconds and go deeper on demand.
5. **Specificity over hype.** Concrete numbers, named tools, honest project status (completed / ongoing) over vague superlatives.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**: body text ≥4.5:1 and large text ≥3:1 contrast against the dark surface, full keyboard navigability, visible focus states, and meaningful alt text on project imagery. Reduced-motion accommodation is not a hard requirement for this project (motion is acceptable), but motion should stay subtle and non-essential to content regardless.
