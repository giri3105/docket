Three open questions living rent-free in my head.

01

Can I build a platform where someone plugs in a functional end-effector and gets zero-shot sim-to-real transfer?

Train entirely in simulation, deploy in the real world without fine-tuning. The sim-to-real gap has always been the expensive part — I want to make that gap disappear for arbitrary end-effectors.

02

In traversability analysis and anomaly detection — how do you handle the scarcity of negative samples?

Collecting "bad" examples — terrain a robot shouldn't cross, faults that rarely happen — is expensive and sometimes dangerous. The field has answers, but I'm digging into which ones hold up in actual deployment.

03

How do you build an aggressive controller for an autonomous vehicle that knows its own uncertainties?

Aggressive doesn't mean reckless — it means extracting maximum performance while staying inside the safe envelope. If the vehicle knows where its model breaks down, can it use that self-knowledge to push harder where it's confident?

end-effector

The part of a robot arm that interacts with the world — grippers, suction cups, tools. Each has a unique geometry and contact model, making generalised sim training hard.

zero-shot transfer

A policy trained in simulation that works in the real world immediately, with zero real-world data. The challenge: simulators are approximations, and real physics doesn't forgive edge cases.

traversability

Given a patch of terrain, can the robot safely move through it? Soft soil, steep slopes, wet surfaces — each needs different risk assessment. Failures are rare and expensive to collect.

anomaly detection

Detecting when something is wrong — a failing component, an unexpected obstacle, an out-of-distribution environment. The catch: you need examples of "wrong" to train on, and those are rare by definition.

aggressive control

Controllers that operate near the vehicle's dynamic limits — high speed, sharp cornering, hard braking. Usually requires precise models and careful tuning. Can learned uncertainty make this safer?

uncertainty-aware

A vehicle that knows "I'm not sure about this surface" or "my model never saw rain" — and uses that self-knowledge to slow down, re-plan, or ask for help. Epistemic humility, in hardware.
